The First Home
Savings Account

What it is, who qualifies, and how to bring it up with clients

Sources: RBC Royal Bank FHSA page · Canada Revenue Agency

The one-line pitch

A tax deduction on the way in.
Tax-free on the way out.

1 · Going in

Every contribution is tax-deductible, just like an RRSP

2 · While invested

Interest, dividends and gains are never taxed

3 · Coming out

Tax-free for a first home, like a TFSA, and never paid back

Eligibility

Who can open one

Adult, 71 or younger

At least 18 and the age of majority in their province (19 here in Nova Scotia), and no older than 71 at Dec 31 of the year opened

Canadian resident

Resident of Canada with a Social Insurance Number

First-time buyer

Hasn’t lived in a home they owned, this year or in the previous 4 calendar years

The spouse rule

Also hasn’t lived in a home their spouse or common-law partner owned in that same window

How it works

How the money moves

1. Contribute

Tax-deductible, claimed this year or saved for a later one

2. Grow

Invested in savings, GICs, funds, ETFs or stocks; growth isn’t taxed

3. Use it

Buy a first home, or roll it into an RRSP (see below)

They buy a first home

Qualifying withdrawal: tax-free, not added to their income, and never paid back

They don’t buy

Direct transfer to their RRSP or RRIF: tax-free, and it doesn’t use RRSP room. Cashing out instead is taxable.

Deadline: Dec 31 of the 15th year after opening, or of the year they turn 71, whichever comes first.

05

Withdrawals

Tax-free withdrawals: six conditions

Still a first-time buyer when they withdraw

Written agreement to buy or build a home in Canada, closing before Oct 1 of the following year

Home not acquired more than 30 days before the withdrawal

Canadian resident from the withdrawal until the purchase

Plans to live in it as their main home within one year

Completes CRA Form RC725 and gives it to us

Couples: each can withdraw from their own FHSA for the same home.

Afterwards: close every FHSA by Dec 31 of the following year.

08

Watch for these

Five things clients get wrong

Waiting until they’re ready to buy

Room only starts once the account is open. Open now and let the room build while they save.

Treating the room like a TFSA

Unused room carries forward only up to $8,000, so they can’t catch up several years at once.

Putting in too much

The excess is taxed at 1% per month until it’s fixed.

Expecting an RRSP-style 60-day window

Contributions count for the calendar year they’re made. January deposits can’t go on last year’s return.

Moving money by cashing it out

Use a direct transfer (Form RC721). A cash withdrawal is taxable, and an FHSA can’t transfer into a TFSA.

Spotting the opportunity

Who to bring it up with

Renters saving to buy

Open now, automate contributions, and match investments to the timeline.

Young adults, new grads

Room starts on opening. Family can gift the cash; deduct it in a higher-income year.

Clients with RRSP savings

A direct RRSP transfer can move savings in. Not deductible; uses FHSA room.

Couples buying together

Each opens their own FHSA; both can use theirs on the same home.

The opener: “Is buying a home part of your plans in the next 15 years?”

11

Takeaways

Three things to remember

1

Open it early. Contribution room only starts the year the account opens.

2

Contribute and deduct. Claim the deduction now, or save it for a higher-income year.

3

Use it or roll it. Tax-free for a first home, or tax-free into an RRSP if they never buy.

Questions?

Backup for Q&A

Quick answers, 1 of 2

QuestionAnswer
More than one FHSA?Yes, but all of a client’s FHSAs share one $8,000 annual room and one $40,000 lifetime limit.
Can it be joint?No. Only the holder contributes and claims the deduction, though anyone can gift them the money.
Does growth use up room?No. Only contributions and RRSP transfers count toward the limits.
FHSA and HBP on one home?Yes, if they meet the conditions for each withdrawal when it’s made.
Can RRSP money go in?Yes, by direct transfer. Not deductible, uses FHSA room, and the RRSP room isn’t restored.
